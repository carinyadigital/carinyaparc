## Why winter suits fencing work

At Carinya Parc, winter on the mid-north coast is not a pause — it is a window. Cooler mornings make long days on boundary lines more manageable. Pasture growth slows, which means we can access fence lines without doing unnecessary damage to recovering ground.

We are still establishing this property. That matters for how we talk about fencing: we are not maintaining a finished system. We are making the farm safe, navigable, and ready for the stock and infrastructure still to come.

## What we are prioritising

On 42 hectares, not every boundary needs the same treatment on the same timeline. Our approach has three layers:

- **Critical boundaries first** — shared lines, creek crossings, and areas where stock or wildlife movement needs clear control.
- **Good-enough materials** — post and wire that will last, without gold-plating sections that may move as our paddock plan evolves.
- **Document as we go** — photos, rough maps, and notes so the next pass is informed, not guessed.

## Lessons from the ground

Fencing on sloping, partially cleared land teaches humility quickly. Old fence lines hide in scrub. Posts that looked straight from the road lean once you string wire. We have learned to walk each section twice — once with eyes, once with measuring tape — before committing to a run.

We also respect the season we are in. Lush green pasture in midwinter here often surprises visitors who expect bare fields. That green ground is soft. We try to minimise repeated vehicle runs and compacted lines along new fence routes.

## What comes next

Spring will shift priorities toward planting and calving preparation. The fence work we complete now should reduce fire-fighting later — fewer temporary gates, fewer "we will fix that section soon" compromises.

We share this as a progress report, not a blueprint. Every property has different soils, neighbours, and budgets. If you are building something from the ground up, we hope the honest version — incomplete, iterative, real — is more useful than a polished before-and-after.

## On the horizon

Dexter cattle and expanded paddock rotation are still ahead of us. Good boundaries are part of that future. For now, we measure success in metres secured, corners that will hold, and a farm map that matches what is actually on the ground.
